The science

A 24‑hour cycle, not a monthly one.

Male hormones follow a circadian rhythm tied to your sleep–wake clock. The pattern is predictable — and when you know it, you can plan your day around it instead of fighting it.

  • 6–8 AMTestosterone peaks. Highest after a full night's sleep — drive, mood and recovery ride high.
  • ~30 minCortisol awakening response. Cortisol surges 30–45 min after you wake to power alertness and energy.
  • PMThe daily decline. Both hormones ease off through the afternoon toward an evening / overnight low.
  • SleepSleep sets the amplitude. Short or broken sleep blunts the morning peak — which is why rest shows up in how you feel.

Sources: Physiology, Testosterone (StatPearls / NCBI) · Physiology, Cortisol (StatPearls / NCBI) · Cortisol awakening response. Educational summary only — see disclaimer below.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Is this a period tracker for men?

Sort of — same idea, different clock. Period apps track a roughly monthly cycle. Men's key hormones follow a daily (circadian) cycle instead, so T‑Tracker focuses on your 24-hour rhythm.

Do I need a blood test or wearable?

No. T‑Tracker works from quick self-reported check-ins on how you feel. It reflects the typical circadian pattern — it doesn't measure your actual hormone levels.

Is my data private?

Privacy is a core principle. This landing-page demo stores nothing beyond your own browser. The full app will be built privacy-first, with your control over your data.

Can it diagnose low testosterone?

No. T‑Tracker is an educational and self-awareness tool, not a medical device. If you're concerned about your hormone levels, talk to a qualified healthcare professional.
